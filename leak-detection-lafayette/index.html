When to look into a possible leak

Leak Detection Lafayette can help when you notice water where it shouldn't be, but can't tell where it's coming from. A damp area, unexplained water sounds, or a change in how a plumbing fixture behaves can be reasons to pay attention. These signs don't always point to the same cause, so avoid guessing at a repair before the source is clearer.

If water is actively spreading, protect belongings and keep people away from slippery areas. If you can safely shut off the relevant water supply, doing so may limit additional water while you arrange help. For a concern that isn't urgent, make a note of when you first noticed it and what has changed since.

What an assessment can involve

A plumber will use the details you share to narrow down where water may be escaping and what needs attention. The visit may include checking the affected area and related plumbing, then explaining the findings and possible next steps. The exact approach depends on what the plumber can observe and the type of plumbing involved.

Before the appointment, it can help to prepare a short checklist:

  • Where you first noticed water or moisture
  • When it appears, and whether it comes and goes
  • Which fixtures or areas seem affected
  • Any steps you've already taken, such as shutting off a supply

Avoid opening walls or taking apart plumbing to search for a leak unless you know how to do so safely. Keep the area accessible and share your notes with the plumber. That gives the visit a useful starting point without risking extra damage.

What should I tell a plumber when I call about a possible leak?

Describe where you noticed water, when it started, and whether it appears constantly or only at certain times. Mention any fixtures or nearby areas that seem involved.

Should I shut off the water before the plumber arrives?

If water is actively spreading and you can safely reach the relevant shutoff, turning it off may help limit additional water. Don't attempt it if the location is unsafe or you're unsure which valve controls the supply.
